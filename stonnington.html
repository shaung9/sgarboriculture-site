City of Stonnington

Tree controls in Stonnington

Stonnington protects significant trees by size rather than by list, which means you do not get to check a register to find out whether your tree is caught. You measure it.

No register to check, so measure the tree

Most councils keep a nominated register of significant trees. Stonnington sets a size instead and lets it catch whatever it catches. There is no list to search, so if your tree is anywhere near either threshold you need to get a tape on it before you assume anything.

Circumference is the measurement around the trunk. A 140 cm circumference works out at roughly a 45 cm diameter, which is a smaller tree than most people picture.

Works inside a protection zone are an offence in their own right

Stonnington charges works within a Tree Protection Zone at 10 penalty units in its own right, separate from any damage to the tree, so you can be liable without the tree being harmed at all. Storing bricks inside the fence would do it. That is why the protection plan and the site induction are worth getting right here.

Neighbourhood Character Overlays

Where an NCO applies, every tree needs a permit, significant or not. The best known is Hedgeley Dene in Malvern East, the first area in Victoria deemed to have a character warranting this kind of protection. Its exemptions are narrow: trees under 5 m tall or under 0.5 m trunk circumference, electric line clearance, immediate risk, and emergency access.

Council's Clause 52.37 page looks incomplete

Stonnington's published summary describes the permit trigger as applying where the land is vacant, or vacant with a new single dwelling proposed. It omits the boundary canopy tree limb that catches developed lots. The state provision is the one that binds, so do not plan around the shorter version on the council page.

What Stonnington does not publish

Stonnington publishes no arborist report checklist and names no minimum qualification, and AS 4970 appears nowhere in its tree material. That is unusual for a council this active on tree protection. It means your report cannot point at a Council template to justify its approach, so it has to state which standard it worked to and why.
