The problem

A request for further information can cost you a month or more

In my experience the same handful of gaps cause most of them, and they are rarely about the trees themselves. They are about what got left out of the writing up.

The impact on the tree is never stated

A thorough description of the trees that never says what the works do to each one. The planner has nothing to assess, so it goes back. Every retained tree needs a sentence connecting the works to that tree and stating the consequence.

The neighbour's tree left out

A tree over the fence whose root zone reaches into your site is squarely in scope, and that neighbour is often the objector. Leaving it out looks like either an oversight or an omission.

Protection written as a generic note

“Standard tree protection in accordance with AS 4970” gives a planner nothing they can write into a condition. They need to know where the fence goes, at what radius, when it goes up, who supervises it and what is allowed inside it. If a council officer cannot enforce it as written, it is not specific enough.

Most of the delay in a tree application comes from the report, not the tree.

Written advice

New provision

Clause 52.37 caught a lot of people out

Amendment VC289 introduced Clause 52.37 Canopy Trees to every Victorian planning scheme on 15 September 2025. It can require a planning permit to remove a tree on a residential site where nothing else would have triggered one.

A tree is caught if it meets all three of: more than 5 m tall, trunk circumference over 0.5 m measured at 1.4 m, and a canopy at least 4 m across. It applies in most residential zones but not Low Density Residential.

The trap is the boundary tree. A canopy tree with any part of its trunk within 6 m of the narrowest street frontage or 4.5 m of the rear boundary loses the exemptions that would otherwise apply on a site with an existing dwelling.

The canopy tree minimum is the part that bites

The clause sets a minimum number of canopy trees for the site, and trees you keep count toward it, so what you have to plant is the shortfall. Sites up to 1,000 m² scale from one tree on a site of 100 m² or less to six between 701 and 1,000 m². Above 1,000 m² it switches to a minimum 20% canopy cover of the site area.

New trees have to reach at least 6 m tall and 4 m wide at maturity, and they need real ground to do it in. That is a constraint on your footprint, and much cheaper to allow for while the plans are still moving.

Common questions

What qualification does a council require on an arborist report?

Several Victorian councils name AQF Level 5, the Diploma of Arboriculture, as the minimum for consulting work, and others ask for a suitably qualified arborist without naming a level. Where a council does name it, a report from an author below that level can be refused on qualification grounds alone.

What changed in AS 4970:2025?

The 2025 revision separated the calculated zone from the applied one. What used to be the calculated TPZ is now the Notional Root Zone (NRZ), and the TPZ is the area protected on site as determined by the project arborist. Diameter at Breast Height became Diameter at Standard Height, still measured at 1.4 metres. Encroachment is now assessed as a proportion of NRZ area rather than radius.

When did Clause 52.37 canopy trees start?

Amendment VC289 introduced Clause 52.37 to all Victorian planning schemes on 15 September 2025. It applies in most residential zones and can require a planning permit to remove a canopy tree even where no other permit is triggered.
