Construction monitoring

Tree conditions on a planning permit usually require an arborist to attend at set stages and certify that protection held. Monitoring is how those conditions get closed out without an argument at occupancy.

Written and signed by Shaun Gerrard, Diploma of Arboriculture (AQF Level 5), and TRAQ qualified through the International Society of Arboriculture.

The permit condition people read too late

Planning permits routinely condition tree protection on arborist attendance at nominated stages, with a final sign off before occupancy. It is written into the permit at endorsement and read properly by almost nobody until the certificate is being chased.

By then the fencing has been down for months, the excavation nobody photographed happened back in week three, and there is nothing on file to certify from. Booking attendance through the build is what keeps that record building as you go.

What a monitoring programme looks like

Before work starts

I check the protection has gone in as specified, photograph it, and sign it off before any machine comes onto the site.

Scheduled attendance

At the hold points in the protection plan, plus any works inside a protection zone. Each visit produces a dated record with photographs.

Exceptions handled on the day

Roots turned up in an excavation, something inside the fence that should not be, a change to the works. The decision gets made and recorded while it can still be influenced.

Completion certification

The documentation that closes the tree conditions out, prepared from the record rather than reconstructed from memory.

Book it against the programme and it costs less

If you give me the programme up front I can book attendances around it and pair them with other work nearby, which keeps the cost down. Last minute call-outs, usually the day council asks why the fence is down, cost more and I cannot always get there the same week.

Common questions

How many visits will I need?

It depends on the permit conditions and the works. Send me the endorsed conditions and I will tell you what they actually require, which is sometimes fewer visits than people assume.

Can you monitor a site somebody else assessed?

Yes, though I will inspect the trees myself before certifying anything about them.

What if protection is found to have failed?

It gets recorded honestly, along with what happened and what can be done about it. A monitoring record that never notes a problem is not worth anything to anybody, least of all you if it is later challenged.
