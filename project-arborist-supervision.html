Service

Project arborist & site supervision

Named on the permit, on site at the hold points, signing off each stage. A hold point is a moment where work stops until the arborist has attended and signed it off. AS 4970:2025 assumes an arborist stays involved for the length of the project.

Fixed quote within one business day · agreed in writing before any work starts

Request a quote How quoting works

What the job involves on site

  • I attend before works start and confirm the protection has gone in as specified
  • I supervise excavation inside protection zones, including hand digging and hydro excavation, which uses pressurised water to wash soil off roots rather than cutting through them
  • I make the call on roots found during excavation, and write down the decision and the reasoning at the time
  • I attend the hold points in the protection plan and sign each one off
  • I issue stage reports where the permit conditions require them
  • I provide the completion documentation that closes out the tree conditions on the permit

Check your permit conditions early

Tree conditions on a planning permit routinely require arborist attendance at set stages and a final sign-off before occupancy. Read them when the permit issues and put the attendances in your programme and your budget. I have seen projects lose three or four weeks because nobody looked at those conditions until the certificate stage.

How it is charged

Supervision is charged hourly against a minimum call-out, quoted in writing before work starts. If your site needs less time than the minimum, I will try to pair the visit with another job nearby so you are not paying for hours you did not use.

Ongoing projects are better handled on a retainer against an agreed number of attendances. Ask and I will price it that way.

Independence

I do not carry out physical tree works of any kind. It matters most here, because when I tell a council that the protection went in correctly and the excavation was supervised, there is no contractor of mine standing behind that statement.

Common questions

What does a project arborist cost in Melbourne?

Attendance is quoted against your programme. Ongoing projects are better arranged as a retainer covering an agreed number of attendances, which usually works out better than booking each one separately.

Do I need a project arborist named before the permit issues?

Often yes. Many Melbourne councils condition tree protection on a named project arborist being engaged, and some require the name at endorsement stage. Check the draft conditions early.

Do you do the tree pruning as well?

No. This is a consulting practice only. I do not prune, remove or climb, which keeps the supervision independent of the works being supervised. I am happy to recommend contractors.

What happens if roots are found during excavation?

You call me and I come and look before anything is cut. Depending on the size and position of the root, I will either specify a clean pruning cut, ask for the works to be adjusted around it, or stop and reassess. Whichever it is, I write down the decision and the reasoning at the time, because council will want that at close-out.
