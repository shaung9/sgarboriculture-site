Clause 52.37 canopy tree reports

Amendment VC289 introduced Clause 52.37 to every Victorian planning scheme on 15 September 2025. It can require a planning permit to remove a tree on a residential site where nothing else would have triggered one, and a lot of projects have been caught by it.

Which trees are caught

A canopy tree must meet all three of these to be protected:

HeightMore than 5 metres above ground level
Trunk circumferenceMore than 0.5 metres, measured at 1.4 m above ground level. Note that is circumference, not diameter
Canopy diameterAt least 4 metres

It applies in the Mixed Use, Township, Residential Growth, General Residential, Neighbourhood Residential and Housing Choice and Transport zones. The Low Density Residential Zone is excluded.

Where the boundary rule catches people

A canopy tree with any part of its trunk within 6 m of the narrowest street frontage or 4.5 m of the rear boundary is a boundary canopy tree, and it does not get the exemptions that otherwise apply on a site with an existing dwelling.

This is the part that catches people out. If there is already a house on the block you might assume every tree on it is exempt. A tree sitting inside those boundary bands is not.

Replacement canopy is the part that bites

Removing a canopy tree brings a replacement obligation with it, and on a tight site that eats into your buildable area.

Site areaReplacement requirement
Up to 100 m²1 tree
101 to 200 m²2 trees
201 to 400 m²3 trees
401 to 700 m²5 trees
701 to 1,000 m²6 trees
Over 1,000 m²20% canopy cover

Replacement trees have to be capable of reaching at least 6 m in height and 4 m of canopy at maturity, and they need real ground to do it in: deep soil of at least 12 m² with a minimum dimension of 2.5 m, or a planter of at least 12 m³ at 0.8 m depth. Find that space on the plan before you commit to a footprint.

What the council may and may not consider

In deciding a Clause 52.37 application the responsible authority must consider only the decision guidelines set out in the clause itself. It cannot bring in state policy, the Municipal Planning Strategy or local planning policy. That works for you and against you depending on what you were planning to argue.

Those guidelines are the tree's contribution to canopy and urban heat, its health and stability, damage to buildings, what the design has done to minimise canopy loss, and replacement planting. An application that speaks to those five things directly is a very different document from a general tree report.

Your overlays still apply on top

Clause 52.37 sits alongside Vegetation Protection, Environmental Significance and Significant Landscape Overlays rather than replacing them. A site can need a permit under both, decided on different tests by different parts of the council. Applications come unstuck when somebody answers one control and assumes it covered the other.

Common questions

When did Clause 52.37 start?

Amendment VC289 introduced it to all Victorian planning schemes on 15 September 2025.

Is a tree exempt if there is already a house on the block?

Only if it is not a boundary canopy tree. A canopy tree with any part of its trunk within 6 m of the narrowest street frontage or 4.5 m of the rear boundary loses that exemption.

Do dead trees need a permit under Clause 52.37?

No. The clause exempts dead trees, emergency and safety works, pruning for maintenance, and trees identified as weed species. Whether a tree is genuinely dead, as opposed to dormant or badly declining, is a question to have assessed. Do not assume it.

What does a Clause 52.37 report cost?

Every job is quoted individually and fixed in writing before I start. Councils usually also want a plan showing the tree to be removed, the existing trees being retained and the proposed replacement planting, so tell me whether you need that and it will be in the quote.

How much replacement canopy will I owe?

On sites up to 1,000 square metres it is a set number of trees scaling from one to six by site area. Above 1,000 square metres the test is a minimum 20% canopy cover of the site.
