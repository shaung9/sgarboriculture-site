Tree Protection Management Plans

The documents that replaced the old tree management plan under AS 4970:2025. A Tree Protection Plan showing what is protected and where, and Tree Protection Specifications setting out exactly what happens inside those zones.

Why generic protection notes get sent back

“Standard tree protection measures to be implemented in accordance with AS 4970” tells a planner nothing they can condition and a site foreman nothing they can follow. It is the single most common weak point in an otherwise sound report.

What a council needs is something it can write into a condition and later check: where the fencing goes, at what radius, when it goes up, who supervises it, what is allowed inside it, and which stages need an arborist on site.

What you get

  • A scaled plan showing each retained tree with its Notional Root Zone, its Structural Root Zone, and the Tree Protection Zone applied on site
  • Fencing type, position and installation timing, tied to the construction programme
  • Ground protection where incursion is unavoidable, specified to a product and a depth
  • Permitted and prohibited activities within each zone, in plain language a site crew can follow
  • Hold points requiring arborist attendance and sign-off
  • Excavation methodology where services or footings encroach
  • Signage and site induction content

The 2025 restructure

Under the previous standard this was typically a single Tree Management Plan. AS 4970:2025 separates it into a Tree Protection Plan and Tree Protection Specifications, alongside the staged reporting sequence. If your council checklist still asks for a TPMP, you will get a document that satisfies that request and is structured to the current standard.

It works better with supervision attached

A plan on its own only helps if someone checks the site against it. Where the permit conditions require attendance, or where the site is tight enough to warrant it, I pair the plan with project arborist attendance at the hold points.

Common questions

Is a Tree Protection Plan the same as a Tree Management Plan?

Broadly it replaces it. AS 4970:2025 separates the single management plan into a Tree Protection Plan and Tree Protection Specifications. Where a council checklist still asks for a TPMP, the documents supplied satisfy that request.

Do I need one if I already have an impact assessment?

Usually yes. The impact assessment establishes what happens to each tree; the protection plan sets out how the retained ones are actually protected during construction. Councils commonly condition the second on the strength of the first.

Can you prepare one for a site someone else assessed?

Yes, though I will need to inspect the trees myself before putting my name to protection measures for them.
