Councils

What each council requires

Almost every council tree page you will find online is written for a homeowner who wants a tree gone. These are written for the person lodging a planning application, which is a different question with different answers.

Councils covered

Yarra

New local law from 1 September 2026, and a threshold set as a diameter.

Clifton Hill, Fitzroy, Collingwood, Richmond, Abbotsford, Carlton North

Darebin

A two part size and height test, and it names the Diploma of Arboriculture as the minimum.

Northcote, Thornbury, Preston, Reservoir, Fairfield, Alphington

Merri-bek

Measures at 1.2 metres, catches pruning over 15%, and has the best Clause 52.37 guidance in Melbourne.

Brunswick, Coburg, Pascoe Vale, Fawkner, Glenroy, Brunswick East

Banyule

The local law does not control private tree removal. The planning scheme does.

Ivanhoe, Heidelberg, Eaglemont, Rosanna, Viewbank, Montmorency

Boroondara

Two separate controls, and most applicants only find one of them.

Camberwell, Hawthorn, Kew, Balwyn, Glen Iris, Canterbury

Stonnington

Significant trees set by size, and bonds that bite harder than most.

Malvern, Toorak, Prahran, Armadale, Glen Iris, South Yarra

Whitehorse

The municipal wide overlay stopped applying in June 2026, and most published advice has not caught up.

Box Hill, Blackburn, Nunawading, Mitcham, Surrey Hills

Monash

No tree local law, by a deliberate council decision. The planning scheme is all there is.

Glen Waverley, Mount Waverley, Clayton, Oakleigh, Chadstone

Bayside

Specific about who is allowed to write the report.

Brighton, Sandringham, Hampton, Black Rock, Beaumaris

Four different measuring heights across nine councils

Across the nine councils on this site a trunk gets measured at four different heights: 100 cm, 120 cm, 140 cm and 150 cm. Clause 52.37 uses 140 cm everywhere, which lines up with AS 4970:2025 but not with several of the local laws.

Trees are wider at the bottom, so 1.0 m and 1.4 m can easily put the same tree either side of a threshold. Where more than one control applies I take the tape to both heights on site rather than converting afterwards, and the report shows which figure answers which test.

Clause 52.37 sits on top of whatever else applies

Since 15 September 2025 every one of these councils also has Clause 52.37 running in parallel with whatever local controls it already had. A site can need a permit under a local law and under the planning scheme, assessed on different tests by different parts of the council. The full explanation is here.
