City of Whitehorse

Tree controls in Whitehorse

Whitehorse was known for SLO9, a municipal wide landscape overlay that caught trees across the whole council. It stopped applying on 16 June 2026, because the state considered a municipal wide local provision superseded by Clause 52.37. A lot of advice still in circulation has not registered that.

The measuring height trap

This is the single most useful thing to know about Whitehorse. The surviving SLO schedules measure trunk circumference at 1 metre above ground level. Clause 52.37 measures at 1.4 metres. AS 4970:2025 measures DSH at 1.4 metres.

On a tree that tapers, and most do, those give different numbers, and a tree can be over the threshold on one measure and under it on another. Measuring at the wrong height is a live source of rejected applications here. Both figures belong in the report.

Council's own site is out of step with its own amendment

Whitehorse's private property tree page still says most trees in Whitehorse are protected by planning overlays and still refers to SLO9. The amendment page says SLO9 stopped applying on 16 June 2026. The amendment is the position that binds. If you have been told your tree is protected on the basis of SLO9, that advice needs rechecking.

What replaced it, in practice

Applicants now fall back on Clause 52.37 plus whichever site specific SLO or VPO schedule covers the land. That is a narrower net in some places and a similar one in others, and it means the first question on a Whitehorse site is which schedule, if any, applies to this particular address.

Best measured canopy data in the belt

Whitehorse publishes lidar measured canopy cover of 25.8% from 2023, with targets of 27% by 2031 and 30% by 2050. That matters for an application because it gives you a documented basis for a canopy argument. Where a proposal retains meaningful canopy, saying so against the council's own published figure is more persuasive than saying so in the abstract.
