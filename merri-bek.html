City of Merri-bek

Tree controls in Merri-bek

Merri-bek measures trunks at 1.2 metres, which is lower than every neighbouring council and lower than the Australian Standard. It also needs a permit to prune more than 15% of a canopy. Both catch people who assumed the rules were the same as next door.

Measurement height

Pruning trigger
A Tree Works Permit is required to prune more than 15% of the tree canopy on private property
Works in a tree protection zone

Significant tree register
Nomination based. A 2021 study identified 492 trees, and owners of 298 properties were notified in May 2022. Published as an interactive map
Report guide

Amendment C189
In operation since 1 September 2021. Adds canopy tree landscaping requirements to developments of two or more dwellings

The 1.2 metre problem

Merri-bek measures trunk diameter at 1.2 metres above ground level. Clause 52.37 measures circumference at 1.4 metres. AS 4970:2025 measures Diameter at Standard Height at 1.4 metres. Yarra, next door, measures at 1.5 m, with a new local law from 1 September 2026 whose definition is worth confirming. Darebin measures at 1.5 m.

Measure 200 mm further down the trunk and you get a bigger number. On a Brunswick plane tree with any flare at the base, that difference alone can decide whether the tree is caught. Council's own trees page gives the 400 mm figure without saying where to measure it, so you have to go to the local law itself for the 1.2 m. Take the tape to both heights while you are on site, because going back for the second one is a wasted trip.

Pruning over 15% needs a permit

Most councils control removal and lopping. Merri-bek also requires a Tree Works Permit to prune more than 15% of a canopy on private property. A routine crown reduction on a mature tree goes past 15% without much effort. It is the control I see breached most often in Merri-bek, almost always by someone who had no idea it existed.

Works inside the protection zone are captured too

Clause 2.5 does not stop at the tree. It captures works within the Tree Protection Zone without authorisation, and the local law defines that zone. For a builder that means the fence line matters legally, not just as good practice, and it is worth having the zone set and documented before machinery arrives.

Merri-bek's Clause 52.37 guidance is the clearest I have read

Merri-bek gets this right where most do not. Its planning pages give the full three part test with the correct 1.4 m measurement height, list all the relevant zones including Residential Growth and Housing Choice and Transport, state the boundary rule correctly at 6 m from the narrowest street frontage and 4.5 m from the rear, and distinguish vacant land from developed land. It also warns that a planning exemption does not remove the local law permit requirement, which is the dual consent trap most builders hit.

One inconsistency worth knowing: Council's own trees on private property page still refers only to Clause 52.17 native vegetation and does not cross reference 52.37. The planning pages are the accurate ones.

The fencing note still calls the 2009 standard current

Technical Note C100.10, dated September 2022, specifies 1.8 m solid chain mesh fencing in concrete feet and hand excavation within the root zone, which is all sound. It also cites the "current Australian Standard 4970-2009". That word is now wrong. Meet the fencing spec, report to the 2025 standard, and state the equivalence.

A local law review is under way

Merri-bek is reviewing its local law through 2026, with adoption targeted for 11 November 2026 and stated scope including stronger protection for trees and vegetation. No new thresholds have been published yet. If you are planning around today's numbers on a long lead time project, check where the review has landed.
