City of Yarra

Tree controls in Yarra

Yarra's General Local Law runs out on 31 August 2026 and the Community Local Law 2026 takes over the next day. Two things change: where you measure the trunk, and whether pruning needs a permit. This is my home council and I have read both versions.

What changes on 1 September 2026

Two things, and the second one is the one that will catch people out.

The measurement height moves from 1.5 m to 1.4 m. That aligns Yarra with Clause 52.37 and with Diameter at Standard Height under AS 4970:2025, which is a sensible tidy up. But on a tree that tapers, and most do, a trunk measured 100 mm lower is a trunk measured wider. Trees that sat just under the 400 mm threshold at 1.5 m may sit above it at 1.4 m. If you measured a tree in Yarra recently and called it exempt, measure it again.

Pruning is added to the prohibition. The current clause covers removing, damaging, destroying or lopping. The new clause 2.3(1) reads: a person must not, without a permit, prune, remove, damage, destroy or lop a significant tree. From 1 September 2026, a tidy up of a 400 mm tree without a permit is an offence in Yarra.

Council's own page has not caught up yet

Yarra's significant trees page still publishes the 1.5 m measurement height. It becomes wrong on 1 September 2026. If someone has told you your tree is under the threshold on the basis of that page, the advice needs rechecking against the new law.

Yarra sets its threshold as a diameter, and Darebin next door sets a circumference

Yarra sets 400 mm diameter. Darebin next door sets 100 cm circumference, which works out at about 318 mm diameter. Merri-bek also uses 400 mm diameter but measures it at 1.2 m instead of 1.4 m. Three adjoining councils and three different tests, so check which one you are actually under before you measure anything.

Yarra's Clause 52.37 information is incomplete

Council's vegetation removal page gives the three part canopy tree test correctly, but it names only the Mixed Use, General Residential and Neighbourhood Residential zones. The clause also applies in the Residential Growth Zone and the Housing Choice and Transport Zone. The page also says nothing at all about boundary canopy trees, the 6 m street frontage rule or the 4.5 m rear boundary rule, and never cites the clause by number.

Do not plan a Yarra site off that page alone. The state provision is what binds.

What Yarra asks for in a report

Less than you would expect. The vegetation removal page lists an arborist report only under what you may also need to provide, from a "suitably qualified arborist". No Australian Qualifications Framework level is named and AS 4970 is not mentioned in either edition.

The landscape plan requirements page is the stronger document and the one to work to. It requires existing trees identified with botanical names, height and canopy spread, the adjoining construction elements that might affect them, and TPZ and SRZ for every retained tree. Where significant vegetation is involved, Council may ask for a Tree Impact Study, which is Yarra's own term for what everyone else calls an impact assessment.

You may need two separate permits

A Yarra site can need a local law permit for the tree and a planning permit for the works. Different parts of Council decide them, on different tests, and getting one does not get you the other. Council states plainly that there is no guarantee a permit will be issued, so build the programme assuming both take time.
