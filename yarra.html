City of Yarra

Tree controls in Yarra

Yarra's General Local Law runs out on 31 August 2026 and the Community Local Law 2026, adopted on 9 June 2026, takes over the next day. This is my home council. Below is what the current law requires, and what I would check before relying on anything about the new one.

The local law changes over on 1 September 2026

Council adopted the Community Local Law 2026 on 9 June 2026. It was gazetted and commences on 1 September 2026, and the General Local Law is revoked the same day.

Under the law as it stands, a significant tree is one whose trunk or trunks measure 400 mm diameter or more at 1.5 m above ground, or at ground level, and you need a permit to remove, damage, destroy or lop one. Council's significant trees page also lists pruning as requiring a permit and publishes a pruning permit fee, so treat pruning a significant tree in Yarra as permit territory now, not from September.

Check the gazetted text before you rely on the new definition

The version of the Community Local Law that went out for public exhibition kept the 1.5 m measurement height and the same four prohibited acts. I have seen it suggested that the height moves to 1.4 m to line up with Clause 52.37 and AS 4970:2025, and that would be a sensible tidy up, but I have not been able to confirm it in the adopted instrument.

If your tree measures anywhere near 400 mm, this matters: 100 mm further down a tapering trunk is a wider measurement, and it can move a tree from outside the control to inside it. Ring Council and get the measurement height confirmed in writing before you commit either way. I will update this page once I have the gazetted text.

Yarra sets its threshold as a diameter, and Darebin next door sets a circumference

Yarra sets 400 mm diameter. Darebin next door sets 100 cm circumference, which works out at about 318 mm diameter. Merri-bek also uses 400 mm diameter, but takes the measurement at 1.2 m rather than 1.5 m. Three adjoining councils and three different tests, so check which one you are actually under before you measure anything.

Yarra's Clause 52.37 information is incomplete

Council's vegetation removal page gives the three part canopy tree test correctly, but it names only the Mixed Use, General Residential and Neighbourhood Residential zones. The clause also applies in the Residential Growth Zone and the Housing Choice and Transport Zone. The page also says nothing at all about boundary canopy trees, the 6 m street frontage rule or the 4.5 m rear boundary rule, and never cites the clause by number.

Do not plan a Yarra site off that page alone. The state provision is what binds.

What Yarra asks for in a report

Less than you would expect. The vegetation removal page lists an arborist report only under what you may also need to provide, from a "suitably qualified arborist". No AQF level is named and AS 4970 is not mentioned in either edition.

The landscape plan requirements page is the stronger document and the one to work to. It requires existing trees identified with botanical names, height and canopy spread, the adjoining construction elements that might affect them, and TPZ and SRZ for every retained tree. Where significant vegetation is involved, Council may ask for a Tree Impact Study, which is Yarra's own term for what everyone else calls an impact assessment.

You may need two separate permits

A Yarra site can need a local law permit for the tree and a planning permit for the works. Different parts of Council decide them, on different tests, and getting one does not get you the other. Council states plainly that there is no guarantee a permit will be issued, so build the programme assuming both take time.
